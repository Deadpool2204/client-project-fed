Shipping Help & FAQ

Clear, simple guide to shipping costs, box size calculations, delivery zones, and taxes.

1. Volumetric Weight vs. Actual Weight

A light package that takes up a lot of space costs as much to fly or drive as a dense package of the same size. To account for this, the shipping industry uses Volumetric (Dimensional) Weight alongside physical Actual Weight.

Service Type Volumetric Divisor Formula 1 Cubic Metre (CBM) Equivalent
Standard Courier & Express Air 5000 (L × W × H in cm) / 5000 200 kg
Commercial Air Cargo (IATA) 6000 (L × W × H in cm) / 6000 167 kg
Surface Bulk / LTL Freight 4000 – 4500 (L × W × H in cm) / 4000 250 kg
Chargeable Weight Rule: Chargeable Weight = max(Actual Weight, Volumetric Weight). Freight rates and additional weight slabs are always calculated against this chargeable weight figure.
2. Domestic Shipping Zone Structure (India)

Logistics transit times and base rates are determined by geographic zone matrices based on Indian postal circles:

  • Zone A — Intra-City (Local): Pickup and delivery within the same city or immediate municipal hub.
    Transit: 24 - 48h
  • Zone B — Intra-State / Regional: Within the same state boundary or adjacent postal circles.
    Transit: 2 - 3 Days
  • Zone C — Metro to Metro: Between major primary logistics hubs (e.g., Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Pune).
    Transit: 1 - 3 Days
  • Zone D — Rest of India (National): Standard inter-state routes between Tier-2 and Tier-3 commercial nodes.
    Transit: 4 - 6 Days
  • Zone E — Special / Remote Regions: North-East states, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar, and Lakshadweep.
    Transit: 5 - 10 Days
3. Cash on Delivery (COD) & Reverse Shipping Rules
Cash on Delivery (COD) Handling Fees

Couriers incur cash collection, audit verification, and digital reconciliation overheads. Under our configured business rules, the COD handling fee is calculated as the higher of:

  • Flat Minimum Fee: ₹45.00
  • Percentage Fee: 2.0% of the collected cash amount
Return to Origin (RTO) Charges

When a package is undelivered after standard delivery attempts (e.g. consignee refused, incorrect address, or consignee unavailable), it is returned to the origin warehouse. RTO carries a freight charge equivalent to 100% of the forward freight rate to cover return transit.

Reverse Pickup / Customer Exchange

Doorstep customer pickups require specialized driver workflows, barcode labeling at pickup, and physical QC verification. Reverse pickups are calculated at 1.25× the standard forward freight rate.

4. Goods and Services Tax (GST) on Logistics

As per statutory provisions under the Indian Goods and Services Tax regime, courier and freight transport services attract 18% GST on total billable freight (including fuel surcharge, docket fees, and handling charges).

Intra-State Shipments (Zone A & B)

Split equally into 9% CGST (Central GST) and 9% SGST (State GST).

Inter-State Shipments (Zone C, D, E & Cargo)

Billed as 18% IGST (Integrated GST).

5. Cross-Border Customs, Duties, & Taxes
Critical Domain Policy: International customs duties and destination import taxes are assessed solely by destination national border agencies upon arrival based on the commodity's HS code and declared value. They are never fabricated or included in standard freight quotations.

Unless a verified commercial contract is signed for Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) clearance, all international parcels are routed under Delivered At Place (DAP / DDU) terms, where duties and local VAT must be settled directly by the consignee prior to final delivery.
